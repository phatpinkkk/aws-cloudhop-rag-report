## Event Information

**Event Name:** AI Agent Build Week  
**Date:** July 25, 2026  
**Location:** 26th Floor, Bitexco Tower, 02 Hai Trieu Street, Saigon Ward, Ho Chi Minh City  
**Role:** Attendee

## Event Content

AI Agent Build Week was a sharing event where teams from the hackathon presented what they had built, how they approached the problem, the AWS services they used, and the difficulties they encountered during development.

The projects covered very different use cases. Some examples included a conversational ordering system for KFC, an adaptive AML/KYT workflow for suspicious transactions, an AI-native tool for solution architects, a crowd-monitoring system combining computer vision with AI agents, and a platform for detecting strategic changes from corporate information.

What made the event interesting was that the teams did not only show their final demos. They also talked about how the ideas changed during development, what failed, the trade-offs they had to make, and what they would do differently next time.

## What Impressed Me Most

I was most impressed by the **ideas and creativity behind the projects**.

Many of the solutions started from problems that were easy to understand, but the teams approached them in ways I would not necessarily have thought of myself. For example, the KFC project went beyond a simple ordering chatbot and included a liveboard that showed orders and AI-agent activity in real time.

![KFC Order Liveboard demo](/images/4-EventsParticipated/event-25.7.png)

*A team demonstrating the KFC Order Liveboard, which tracks orders and AI-agent activity in real time.*

Other teams explored problems such as detecting suspicious financial activity, helping solution architects generate initial cloud designs, and monitoring crowd conditions using computer vision and agentic AI. Seeing such different applications built around AWS made the possibilities of combining cloud services and AI feel much more concrete.

## What I Learned

One thing I took away from the presentations was that a good AI project is not only about having a strong model.

The teams had to think about the complete product: what problem they were solving, what data was available, which AWS services made sense, how users would interact with the system, how much it would cost, and whether the idea could realistically be completed within the available time.

I also liked hearing about the difficulties behind the demos. Teams talked openly about limited time, unfamiliar AWS services, latency, cost, integration issues, and even ordinary teamwork problems. That made the projects feel much more realistic than simply seeing a polished final result.

## Experience and Reflection

By this point in my own internship, my team was also moving from experimentation toward a complete AWS application, so many of the presentations felt especially relevant.

The event reminded me that there is rarely only one way to use AI or AWS to solve a problem. What impressed me most was how differently each team interpreted its problem and turned that idea into something they could actually demonstrate.

It made me want to become better not only at implementing AI techniques, but also at identifying useful problems and thinking more creatively about how technology can be turned into a practical solution.
