Participating in **AWS First Cloud AI Journey** was different from a conventional internship where interns mainly receive assigned tasks from a company. The program felt closer to a guided technical learning journey: I was expected to study AWS services, explore a project with my team, work through technical problems independently, and gradually turn what I learned into a complete application and report.

For me, this structure was one of the most valuable parts of the experience. It gave me enough direction to know what I needed to achieve, while still leaving room to make technical decisions, test different approaches, make mistakes, and learn from them.

### Overall Evaluation

**1. Working Environment**

The learning environment was open and flexible. I did not feel that I had to follow a fixed solution for every task, which gave me room to experiment and approach problems in my own way.

This was especially useful during the CloudHop RAG project, where many decisions could not be answered by following a single tutorial. Our team had to compare retrieval approaches, investigate evaluation issues, make trade-offs between quality and deployment constraints, and decide how the different AWS components should fit together.

I also appreciated that the program encouraged independent learning. At the same time, this meant that managing my own progress was important, because there was less day-to-day supervision than in a traditional workplace.

**2. Support from Mentor / Team Admin**

The mentor and FCAJ team provided useful guidance throughout the program, particularly when we needed clarification about project requirements, report structure, or the expected direction of our work.

What I appreciated most was that the support usually came in the form of guidance rather than complete solutions. I still had to research the problem, try different approaches, and understand why something worked or failed. This made the learning process more meaningful than simply following a predefined set of steps.

The mentor's feedback was also useful when our team needed to confirm whether the project, proposal, workshop, and report were moving in the right direction.

**3. Relevance of Work to Academic Major**

The program was highly relevant to my Computer Science background, especially because my main interest is in AI and machine learning.

The CloudHop RAG project allowed me to apply concepts that I was already familiar with, such as text embeddings, retrieval, evaluation, and machine learning experimentation, while also introducing areas that I had less experience with, particularly AWS infrastructure and deployment.

This combination was useful because it connected what I had learned academically with the engineering work required to turn an AI experiment into an application that other people could actually use.

**4. Learning & Skill Development Opportunities**

The strongest learning opportunity for me came from working on the project itself. I learned much more by having to solve a real problem than I would have from studying the AWS services separately.

On the AI side, I gained more experience with lexical and semantic retrieval, hybrid retrieval, multi-hop reasoning, benchmark preparation, and evaluation. I also became much more careful about validating data and experimental setups before trusting the resulting metrics.

On the engineering side, I developed a better understanding of how storage, vector search, backend services, APIs, permissions, and frontend deployment fit together in an AWS application.

Another important lesson was learning to work within practical constraints. The strongest experimental configuration was not always the most suitable configuration for deployment, so the project required us to think about latency, available resources, cost, and reliability in addition to retrieval quality.

**5. Team Culture & Collaboration**

Working with my team was an important part of the experience. We gradually divided responsibilities based on our strengths, which allowed us to make progress on retrieval, evaluation, backend development, frontend integration, and AWS deployment in parallel.

My main responsibility was retrieval and evaluation, but the project could not be completed by treating each part independently. We still needed to discuss interfaces, share results, understand how changes in one component affected another, and validate the final system together.

I especially valued the final integration stage because it made the connection between our individual work much clearer. The retrieval pipeline I had been evaluating was no longer just an experiment; it had become one component of a complete application.

### Additional Questions

**What did you find most satisfying during your internship?**

The most satisfying part was seeing the project gradually move from an idea and a set of experiments into a working end-to-end application.

Most of my work was focused on retrieval and evaluation, so I spent a lot of time looking at datasets, retrieval candidates, metrics, and failure cases. Seeing that work eventually connect with the backend, AWS services, API, and frontend made the earlier experimentation feel much more meaningful.

I also found it rewarding when we were able to understand why something was not working rather than simply changing configurations until the result improved. Some of the most useful lessons came from debugging evaluation problems, inspecting retrieval failures, and understanding the trade-offs between a stronger experimental pipeline and a practical deployed version.

**What do you think the program should improve for future interns?**

I think the program could benefit from a few more technical checkpoints during the project period.

The flexibility of FCAJ is valuable because interns are encouraged to explore independently, but it can also mean that a team may spend a significant amount of time moving in one direction before receiving feedback. A short technical review around the middle of the project could help teams confirm their architecture, scope, evaluation approach, and deployment plan before they invest too much time in implementation.

I also think more opportunities for interns to share what they are building with one another would be useful. Different teams are likely to encounter different AWS services and technical problems, so short project-sharing sessions could allow interns to learn from experiences outside their own project.

**If recommending the program to a friend, would you suggest they join? Why or why not?**

Yes, especially for someone who is comfortable learning independently and wants practical exposure to AWS.

I would explain that FCAJ is not the kind of internship where every task is clearly defined in advance. A large part of the experience depends on how much effort the intern is willing to put into researching, experimenting, and developing the project.

For someone who prefers being given exact instructions for every step, that may be challenging. For me, however, the freedom to explore a technical problem with my team while still having guidance from the program made the experience worthwhile.

### Suggestions & Expectations

**Do you have any suggestions to improve the internship experience?**

One improvement I would suggest is adding a lightweight mid-project review where each team briefly presents its current architecture, progress, major difficulties, and next steps.

This would not need to be a formal presentation. Even a short technical discussion could help teams identify problems earlier and also create more interaction between interns working on different projects.

I would also find it useful to have more practical sharing sessions around topics such as AWS architecture decisions, deployment debugging, cost management, and lessons learned from previous FCAJ projects. These are areas where seeing real examples is often more useful than only reading documentation.

**Would you like to continue this program in the future?**

Yes. I would be interested in continuing with more advanced AWS and AI topics, especially projects that go further into deploying and operating AI systems in the cloud.

This internship also showed me that cloud infrastructure and production engineering are areas where I still want more hands-on experience. A future FCAJ activity that combines AI development with deeper AWS architecture, monitoring, deployment, and optimization would be particularly useful for me.

**Any other comments?**

Overall, FCAJ gave me a better understanding of the gap between developing an AI method and building a complete AI system.

I started the program expecting to learn more about AWS and RAG, but the most useful part was seeing how many different decisions are involved once an experiment has to become an actual application. Data preparation, evaluation, retrieval quality, latency, cloud resources, APIs, permissions, deployment, and teamwork all affected the final result.

I appreciate the guidance from the FCAJ team and mentor throughout the program, as well as the freedom our team had to explore the project in our own way. The experience gave me a clearer idea of the technical areas I am already comfortable with and the skills I still want to develop further as an AI engineer.
